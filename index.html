Venkatesh Kabra, PhD

Energy systems researcher · Mechanical Engineering

I'm a battery researcher who likes to understand how things work from the inside out. I earned my B.Tech (Honors) in Mechanical Engineering with an Electrical minor from IIT Bombay (Jul 2014 – May 2018), then entered the direct PhD program in Mechanical Engineering at Purdue University (Aug 2018 – Dec 2023), supported by the Ross Fellowship. There I worked with Prof. Partha P. Mukherjee on lithium-ion batteries: their characterization, aging, thermal behavior, and state estimation from measurements. My work included a reduced-order cell model, an in-operando pulse voltammetry method for estimating state of charge and health, and thermal-abuse testing of commercial cells. I also have a pending patent application on impedance-based state estimation.

Before that, I interned at Washington University in St. Louis (May – Jul 2017) on perovskite solar cells with Prof. Supratim Biswas, which first introduced me to renewable energy research. Today I'm most interested in combining physics-based modeling with data-driven methods to understand and predict battery behavior.

Research Interests

  • Cell characterization

    Electrochemical testing and measurement methods for lithium-ion cells.

  • Thermo-electrochemical and degradation modeling

    Mesoscale and continuum models of coupled heat transfer, electrochemistry, and aging.

  • Thermal safety and abuse

    Thermal runaway behavior and abuse response of battery cells.

  • State estimation

    State of charge (SOC), state of health (SOH), and temperature estimation, including impedance-based approaches.

  • Data-driven modeling

    Machine learning applied to battery data and prediction.
